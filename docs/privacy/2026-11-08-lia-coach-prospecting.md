## 2. Step 1: the interest

- **The interest:** FitPal's, in finding its first coaches. It is a one-person product with no
  audience yet. Coaches are the product's buyers, and a DM is how they are reached in their own
  market (`2026-11-funnel-and-promotion.md`).
- **Lawful:** direct marketing is an interest the GDPR recognises (recital 47). The message is
  about the coach's own work, not a consumer offer.
- **Clear and present:** it is written down here and in the notice, and the outreach runs now. It
  is not a speculative future use.

**Result:** a legitimate interest.

## 3. Steps 2 and 3: necessity and balancing

### 3.1 Necessity

- **Could it be done with less data?** The list keeps only what the opener and the follow-up rule
  need. The follower count checks the list's 5k–100k range (DM doc §3). The bio link and the app
  named are what the message talks about. **`niche` has no use written down yet:** name one (for
  example, choosing the proof in follow-up 2) or drop the column (to verify, owner). The dates enforce the two-follow-up limit and the 6-month cut. No
  personal account, no contact details off the profile, no tool that collects profiles in bulk.
- **Could it be done without a list?** No, not safely. Without a list, a coach who said "stop"
  could be written to again by the next search done by hand. The do-not-contact line is the
  safeguard that needs the list.
- **Is there a less intrusive way?** Content on @fitpal.ma, SEO pages and the "comment APP" flow
  run alongside this (`2026-11-content-calendar.md`), but they reach coaches who already know us. A
  first message by hand is the least intrusive way to reach coaches who don't. An ad audience built
  from the same coaches would process more data, through Meta's targeting, and say less.

### 3.2 The coach's reasonable expectations

- The profiles are business accounts set up to be found by people who want something from the
  coach. Messages about the coach's work are what a business account is for.
- The opener names one of the coach's own posts and is sent by a person, so it reads as what it
  is, not as bulk mail.
- A tool vendor writing to a coach about software for their coaching is within what a professional
  can expect. A message about something else (a consumer product, a personal matter) would not
  be, and isn't sent.

### 3.3 Impact on the coach

- **Nature of the data:** public, professional, no special categories (Art. 9). No health data:
  the niche records what the coach sells, not anything about their body.
- **Intrusion:** one message, landing in Instagram's message requests, and at most two follow-ups,
  which Instagram allows only once the request is accepted (rule 3). Nothing after a no.
- **Vulnerability:** adults acting as businesses. A coach under 18 is possible at 5k followers, so
  the list leaves off any account that looks like it belongs to one (DM doc §3, added with this
  assessment). When unsure, it is left off.
- **Power:** none. The coach can ignore the message, block the account, or say "stop".
- **Consequences:** none beyond the messages. No profiling, no automated decision, no sharing, no
  newsletter.

### 3.4 Safeguards that make the balance tip

1. The short notice in message 1: what is kept, why, where the full notice is, how to say no;
   and a "stop" line in each follow-up.
2. The full notice on fitpal.ma, published on 8 November 2026, linked from the sending account's
   bio (to set before the first send).
3. "Stop" is honoured at once, and the row is cut down within 7 days.
4. At most two follow-ups, then the row is closed.
5. By hand, at 30 a day, with no automation (rule 5), so the volume stays human.
6. Only the founder sees the list. It is never added to a newsletter, sold or shared.
7. Retention as in section 1, reviewed at each Monday review.
8. Erasure on request the same day, keeping a handle-only do-not-contact line that can also be
   deleted.
9. Accounts that look like they belong to someone under 18 are left off.

The notice publishes 2, 3, 4, 6, 7 and 8; the messages carry 1; 5 and 9 are the DM doc's own
rules.

**Result:** the coach's interests do not override FitPal's, as long as every safeguard above holds.
If one is dropped (automation, more follow-ups, a list shared with a partner), this assessment no
longer holds.

## 4. The rules on unsolicited electronic messages

A legitimate interest covers the GDPR. A separate rule in each country decides whether an
unsolicited commercial electronic message needs prior consent, and it applies whatever the GDPR
basis. **Counsel's answer (reported by the owner on 14 November 2026): an Instagram DM is not an
electronic message under these rules.** So none of them applies to the DMs, and only the GDPR
does, for coaches in the EU: FitPal offers its service to them, so the GDPR reaches it from
outside the EU (Art. 3(2)(a)). The table keeps the reading of 8 November 2026 that led to the
question, with the result after counsel's answer:

| Country | Rule | Covers an Instagram DM? (8 November reading) | Exception for a professional? | Result after counsel |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **France** | CPCE art. L34-5; "courrier électronique" defined in LCEN art. 1 (any text message stored on the network or the recipient's device until they collect it) | Likely yes: the definition is technology-neutral, and commentators read it as covering private messages on social networks ([CMS](https://cms.law/en/fra/legal-updates/prospection-commerciale-et-rgpd-vers-un-durcissement-du-cadre-juridique)) | **Yes.** The CNIL allows prospecting a professional without consent if the message relates to their profession and they can object easily ([CNIL](https://www.cnil.fr/fr/la-prospection-commerciale-par-courrier-electronique)) | **May send.** The opt-out stays in every message (safeguard 1) |
| **Spain** | LSSI art. 21.1: commercial messages "por correo electrónico u otro medio de comunicación electrónica equivalente" need prior request or express consent | Likely yes ("equivalente") | **No.** Art. 21.2 excepts only a prior contract for similar products ([Ley 34/2002](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2002-13758)) | **May send** (the rule doesn't cover a DM) |
| **Belgium** | Code de droit économique art. XII.13 and the royal decree of 4 April 2003 | Likely yes | Only impersonal company addresses, not an individual coach's own account | **May send** (the rule doesn't cover a DM) |
| **Morocco** | Law 09-08 art. 10. The CNDP's factsheet: prior consent, **and a CNDP declaration before starting**; it covers "les différentes formes de messagerie électronique" ([CNDP](https://www.cndp.ma/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/fiche_CNDP_Prospection_RT_fr.pdf)) | Yes, on the factsheet's wording | **No.** The only exception is a prior sale of similar products | **May send** (09-08 doesn't apply to FitPal, section 4.1) |

### 4.1 The sender is in Morocco

Version 1 asked whether law 09-08 governs this list because FitPal's controller is in Morocco
(the legal notice's « Éditeur »). If it did, that would have meant art. 10's prior consent, a CNDP
declaration before prospecting, and an art. 43/44 basis for the Sheet's transfer to the US.
**Counsel's answer (reported by the owner on 14 November 2026): law 09-08 does not apply to
FitPal.** So none of those three is needed for this list.
